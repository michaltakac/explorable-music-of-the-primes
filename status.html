Reference

Status, sources, glossary

This page keeps three things apart: mathematics that is established, the claims the manuscript makes, and what the current text of the manuscript contains.

Established

  • The prime number theorem (Hadamard, de la Vallée Poussin, 1896) and the explicit formulas of Riemann, von Mangoldt and Weil that tie primes to zeros.
  • The mirror symmetry of zeta and of all Dirichlet and Hecke L-functions, including Tate's adelic proof (1950).
  • Connes' trace formula for finitely many places (1998) and Meyer's global trace formula (2005), which is equivalent to Weil's explicit formula.
  • Numerical verification of the Riemann hypothesis for all zeros of zeta up to height $3\cdot10^{12}$ (Platt and Trudgian, 2021).
  • The analogue of the Riemann hypothesis for curves over finite fields (Weil, 1948) and for varieties over finite fields (Deligne, 1974).

The Riemann hypothesis and its generalizations for number fields are not on this list. They have been open since 1859.

Claimed in the manuscript

  • A trace formula on Hardy-Sobolev completions of the space of functions on the idele class group, in which only zeros within a chosen distance of the critical line contribute (Theorem 12.5.1.1).
  • As a consequence, the generalized Riemann hypothesis for the L-function of every unitary Hecke character of every number field. This includes the Riemann zeta function, all Dirichlet L-functions and all Dedekind zeta functions.
  • Chapter 14 also discusses a bound on the multiplicity of zeros away from the centre, by the degree of the number field.

The manuscript says its method does not extend to function fields over finite fields, and that the higher-rank case (the L-functions of automorphic forms on $GL_n$) is the subject of ongoing work. It does not claim that zeros are simple.

The state of the text

These notes describe the PDF dated 20 November 2026, which has 940 pages. They are a description of what is on the page. They are not a referee's report, and nothing here establishes that any step of the argument is right or wrong.

The announcement

The PDF was released on 20 November 2026 in a post by Ravi Vakil, Hurried Announcement: Alessandro Masullo GRH, on his blog Math 216: Foundations of Algebraic Geometry. The post calls the work a proposed proof. It says that Brian Conrad has examined it closely for months, that his feedback is being incorporated, and that it is so far looking good. It says the original plan was to spend a year polishing the writing before announcing anything, and that the draft is being released now in the hope that readers will check it, formalize it and fill any small gaps. It also says the proof was written without AI, and that a longer account will follow.

The author's own account

The acknowledgements (page 6) say the work began in the author's first year of graduate school at Stanford under Brian Conrad and took nearly twelve years. They say the mathematics is complete, that the writing still needs to be filled in at several places, that the introduction is unfinished and will be replaced, and that the author and Conrad know of several glitches that were being addressed. They describe a plan to spend a year polishing the paper, and say the present version was released early on advice. The introduction includes excerpts from a letter sent to Peter Sarnak in October 2026.

Two places where the text disagrees with itself

  • The trace formula appears in two forms. Theorem 12.5.1.1 sums over zeros inside a strip around the critical line, for windows that vanish on elements of size 1. Theorem 13.3.1.1 sums over zeros on the critical line with multiplicity capped at the degree of the field, under different conditions on the window.
  • The introduction (section 1.4.6) says Weil's positivity criterion is not used. Section 14.1.2 describes the theorem on zeros as a consequence of the trace formula combined with a positivity criterion, citing a theorem whose reference is unresolved.

These read as traces of different stages of drafting. A final version would have to settle which statements are meant.

Weighing a claim like this

A proof of a famous conjecture is accepted when specialists have checked every step and the checks have held up, which for a text of this length takes months or years. Unfinished sections do not show that an argument is wrong. They mean it cannot yet be checked in full. Serious attempts on the Riemann hypothesis have failed before at a single step, so caution is the normal response and says nothing against this author.

The ideas explained in this guide are worth understanding whichever way the checking goes: the explicit formula, the adelic picture, trace formulas, and the proposal to compute the prime side one prime at a time in a normed space.

Glossary

Adele
A list with one entry for each ruler of a number field: one for each ordinary notion of size and one for each prime. All but finitely many entries are integers of their kind.
Character
A rule that tags numbers with points on the unit circle and respects multiplication. Dirichlet characters tag whole numbers by their remainder. Hecke characters tag the primes of a number field, and are the same thing as characters of the idele class group.
Critical line
The vertical line of complex numbers with real part ½. It is the fixed line of the mirror symmetry of an L-function.
Explicit formula
An identity with a sum over primes on one side and a sum over zeros on the other, valid for a whole class of test functions.
Hardy-Sobolev space
The manuscript's name for a Hilbert space of functions on the idele class group, defined by a weight that forces decay at rate $a$ and smoothness of degree $b$. After the Mellin transform its members live on a strip of half-width $a$.
Idele, idele class group
An idele is an adele whose entries can all be inverted. The idele class group is the ideles with two counted as equal when they differ by a number of the field. Its elements have a size, a positive real number.
Kernel
The table of weights of a smear: how much of the value at $y$ ends up at $x$.
L-function
A function built from a character as a sum over whole numbers, or equally as a product over primes. Zeta is the L-function of the trivial character.
Mellin transform
The decomposition of a function on the positive numbers into pure powers $t^{s}$. It is the Fourier transform written for multiplication.
Number field
A number system obtained from the rationals by adjoining roots of polynomial equations, such as $\sqrt{-1}$ or $\sqrt{2}$.
Place
One ruler of a number field: either a prime or an ordinary notion of size.
Schauder basis
A sequence of functions such that every function in the space is a unique infinite combination of them. With a basis, the trace of an operator can be read off as a sum of diagonal entries.
θ-function (adelic)
What you get by adding up the values of a function on the adeles over all nonzero numbers of the field. Its Mellin transform is a multiple of an L-function.
Trace
For a matrix, the sum of the diagonal entries, which equals the sum of the eigenvalues. For a smear, the total of the kernel along the diagonal.
Trace formula
An equation obtained by computing one trace in two ways.
Zero
A value of $s$ where an L-function equals 0. The generalized Riemann hypothesis says every zero in the critical strip lies on the critical line.
